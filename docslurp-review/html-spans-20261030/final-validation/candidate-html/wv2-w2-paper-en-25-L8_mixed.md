# Thermal activation and operational stability of Thermomyces lanuginosus lipase

# immobilized on amine-functionalized SBA-15 silica

## Abstract

Lipase B–type hydrolases are workhorses of fine-chemical synthesis, yet their use above 50 °C is limited by rapid unfolding. We immobilized Thermomyces lanuginosus lipase (TLL) on SBA-15 silica grafted with 3-aminopropyl groups (pore diameter

- 8.6 nm) and compared the kinetics and thermal stability of the carrier-bound enzyme with the free enzyme between 30 and 70 °C. Immobilization raised the apparent Km toward p-nitrophenyl butyrate by 45 % at 30 °C, consistent with mild internal diffusion limitation, but lowered the apparent activation energy from 52.4 to 44.1 kJ mol⁻¹. First-order deactivation at 65 °C slowed 5.5-fold, extending the half-life from 30.0 to 165.0 min. After ten 4-h batches of tributyrin hydrolysis the immobilized preparation retained 86.9 % of its initial activity with less than 2 % protein leaching. These results support SBA-15-NH₂ as a carrier for continuous lipase processes operated at 55–65 °C.

Keywords: lipase; enzyme immobilization; mesoporous silica; thermal deactivation; Arrhenius kinetics

### 1. Introduction

Enzymes offer selectivity that is hard to match with chemical catalysts, and the steady improvement of protein engineering has widened their industrial reach [1]. Lipases (triacylglycerol hydrolases, EC 3.1.1.3) are among the most widely used enzymes because they accept a broad range of esters and remain active in organic solvents. The lipase from Thermomyces lanuginosus (TLL) is produced cheaply at scale and is used in detergents, oil modification and biodiesel production [3].

Free lipases are, however, difficult to recover and lose activity above roughly 50 °C. Immobilization on a solid carrier addresses both problems: the biocatalyst can be filtered off and reused, and multipoint attachment or confinement can rigidify the protein against unfolding [2, 9]. Ordered mesoporous silicas such as SBA-15 are attractive carriers because their pore diameter (5–10 nm) can be matched to the size of the enzyme and their surface is easily functionalized [4, 5].

Previous work from our group showed that amine grafting increases TLL loading on SBA-15 by electrostatic attraction at pH 7.5, where the carrier is positively charged and the enzyme (pI ≈

- 4.4) carries a net negative charge [6]. Whether the same preparation also improves operational stability at elevated temperature was not examined. Thermal behaviour is decisive for process design: a 10 °C increase in operating temperature roughly doubles the reaction rate and lowers the viscosity of oil substrates, but only pays off when the catalyst survives long enough to amortize its cost [12, 16].

Here we report a systematic comparison of free and immobilized TLL between 30 and 70 °C. We determine Michaelis–Menten constants at five temperatures, fit Arrhenius parameters, measure first-order deactivation at three temperatures and test reusability in repeated batches. Structural information from differential scanning fluorimetry, carried out at Hanbit National University, is used to interpret the stabilization mechanism [7].
