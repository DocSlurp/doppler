Dear fellow shareholders,

When we opened our first store in a converted feed warehouse in Hood River in 2008, we promised to sell only what we would carry ourselves. Seventeen years later that promise still decides what goes on our shelves, and in 2025 it carried us to record net revenue of $346.3 million, up 9.6% from the prior year.

Three things stood out. First, our stores kept their edge: comparable-store sales rose 6.2% even as we absorbed a wet spring that delayed the camping season by almost a month in the Pacific Northwest. Second, online revenue grew 22.3%, helped by buy-online-pick-up-in-store, which now accounts for 31% of web orders. Third, we took our first steps abroad, and the results surprised even the optimists in the room.

Our Kyoto Arashiyama store (京都嵐山店), opened in April 2023, finished its second full year profitable, and in September we opened in Sapporo’s Ōdōri district (札幌大通) with a rental program for winter gear designed with our partner Hokkaidō Yama Kōbō (北海道山工房). Calgary and Vancouver continue to build steadily.

We also made choices that cost us in the short term. We exited two leases in suburban malls where foot traffic no longer justified the rent, and we raised starting pay in every store to at least $19.50 an hour. Gross margin still expanded by 110 basis points because full-price sell-through improved and we cut freight costs by consolidating inbound containers through the Port of Tacoma.

Looking ahead, we plan to open four stores in 2026, all in towns where our loyalty members already drive more than an hour to shop with us. We will keep our balance sheet conservative: we ended the year with $48.2 million in cash and repaid $12.0 million of our term loan.

Thank you for your trust, and see you on the trail.

Margaret “Peg” Ostrowski Chair and Chief Executive Officer Portland, Oregon, February 24, 2026## Business review

Stores. Retail sales were driven by apparel and footwear, which together made up 54% of store revenue. Hard goods were flat, with strength in packrafts and trekking poles offset by weaker tent sales after two very strong years.

Online. Our rebuilt website went live in March. Conversion rose from 2.4% to 2.9%, and average delivery time fell to 2.6 days. Returns stayed below 9% of online revenue thanks to more detailed size guides.

Repair and resale. The Second Ascent program, which buys back used gear for store credit, processed 27,300 items. Resale revenue is included in retail stores and carried a gross margin of 61%.

## Management’s discussion of results

Gross profit increased to $159.0 million, or 45.9% of net revenue, compared with 44.8% in fiscal 2024. The improvement reflects lower inbound freight, fewer clearance markdowns and a higher share of private-label apparel.

Figure 3: Scope 1 and 2 emissions, 2021–2025

Emissions from our stores and distribution center fell 11.1% in 2025, mainly because 21 stores now buy renewable electricity through utility green-tariff programs. Our target is a 50% reduction from the 2021 baseline by 2030; we are currently 30.6% below it.

Packaging: 94% of online orders shipped in recycled or reusable mailers (2024: 81%). Product: 38% of apparel units used recycled or responsibly sourced fibers.
